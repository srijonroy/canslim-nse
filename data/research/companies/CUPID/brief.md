## The business in 5 lines
Cupid makes male and female condoms, lubricants and diagnostic test kits (HIV, Hepatitis B, syphilis). 59% of revenue is exports to 125+ countries, much of it through WHO/UNFPA, Global Fund and other institutional tenders (2026-05_ppt p.4, p.12).
FY26 revenue was ₹358 crore and profit ₹108 crore, up from ₹183 crore and ₹41 crore. Q1 FY27 brought ₹155 crore of revenue and ₹44 crore of profit (facts.md).
A new consumer (FMCG) business — condoms, face wash, talc, body mist sold in Indian shops — went from close to nothing to ₹84–122 crore in FY26 (the two documents disagree on the figure, see Red flags).
The company is investing ₹331.5 crore in **Baazar Style Retail** (a 260-store value-fashion chain) through warrants; it expects that chain to sell its FMCG products and add ₹150 crore of Cupid revenue in FY27 (2026-05_ppt p.5, p.7).
Guidance: FY27 revenue ₹600 crore and profit ₹180 crore; FY29 ₹1,150 crore and ₹390 crore (2026-05_ppt p.13).

## Valuation (not scored)
Market cap about ₹42,000 crore (₹312 a share). On TTM profit of ₹137 crore that's a **P/E of 306**; on management's FY27 profit guidance (₹180 crore) it's **233×**; on its own FY29 guidance (₹390 crore) it's still about **108×**. That's about 93× TTM sales.
The market cap was ₹16,301 crore on 15 May 2026 (annual report), so the stock is up about 2.6× in 4.5 months. **The price already assumes growth well beyond management's own targets for several years.** Even if every guidance number is hit, the stock could fall if the multiple returns to normal.

## Red flags
1. **Promoter pledge**: about 3.42% of shares, described as "reducing" (annual_report_2026 highlights). Small, but it's a red flag on the checklist → conviction capped at 2.
2. **FMCG revenue differs between documents**: annual report says "₹121.61 Cr FMCG revenue in FY2026"; presentation says "New FMCG Products ₹84.26 Cr". Possibly different definitions, but it needs an explanation.
3. **Investing in a customer**: ₹331.5 crore (about 75% of Cupid's equity) into Baazar Style Retail, which is then expected to generate ₹150 crore of Cupid sales. No related-party link is disclosed, but money going into a buyer that then buys your products deserves scrutiny: check Baazar Style's purchases from Cupid and its own results.
4. **No earnings calls since Jan 2024**: management no longer takes analysts' questions in public.
5. **Retail frenzy**: shareholders up 2× in 3 quarters and the stock up 2.6× in 4.5 months.

Checked and clean: auditor's fraud reporting, qualified opinion / emphasis of matter (none), related-party transactions (none disclosed), the equity jump (a bonus issue).

## Bull case / bear case
- **Bull:** A near-monopoly niche: few WHO-prequalified condom makers exist, it will be the only Indian nitrile maker, and margins are around 35–39%. Capacity is set to rise 2.5×, and the FMCG business adds a second engine. Management has beaten every number it gave since 2023.
- **Bear:** The price already assumes several years beyond the guidance. The business depends on donor-funded tenders that can be cut. Cash is tied up in 6+ months of inventory. The FMCG growth partly comes from a retailer Cupid is putting ₹331 crore into. Management has stopped taking analysts' questions.

## I'm wrong if
- Q2 FY27 revenue is below ₹140 crore, or FY27 tracks below ₹550 crore.
- FMCG revenue through Baazar Style turns out to be a large share of FMCG growth while Baazar's own sales don't grow.
- The promoter pledge goes up.
- A major tender buyer (UNFPA, Global Fund) cuts orders.
- The Palava capacity isn't commissioned by Mar 2027.
