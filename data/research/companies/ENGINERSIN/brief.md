## The business in 5 lines
- Engineers India (EIL) is a government-owned (President of India 51.32%, AR p.244) engineering consultant for oil, gas and refining. It also does turnkey (LSTK) projects, mostly on a cost-plus "open book" basis.
- It earns fees on project management and engineering (consultancy, segment margin 22-31%) and thin margins on turnkey (3-8%, with one-off spikes). Customers are mostly OMCs (IOC, HPCL, BPCL), ONGC, and increasingly overseas (ADNOC, Dangote).
- Size: FY26 standalone turnover Rs 3,850 crore, consolidated net profit Rs 692 crore (facts.md). Zero debt (borrowings Rs 16 crore) and large cash and investments.
- What changed in the last 2 years: overseas consultancy order book rose from Rs 1,933 crore (Mar 2025) to Rs 11,418 crore (Sep 2026), led by Dangote (Nigeria, Ethiopia, Kenya) and ADNOC. Consultancy is now 75% of the Rs 20,420 crore order book (docs/2026-10_ppt.txt p.13).
- Newer areas it is pushing: infrastructure (data centres, 45% of business income per management), nuclear balance-of-plant, coal gasification. These are unproven.

## Red flags
- None confirmed. Checked: promoter holding (51.3%, steady; government), pledge (none found), auditor opinion (unqualified; Emphasis of Matter only), equity dilution (none), debt (almost nil).
- Suspected 1: Rs 410 crore contractor claim against EIL pending in the Supreme Court (EoM, Note 49). The company counter-claims Rs 129 crore. Size is about 6% of net worth; outcome unknown.
- Suspected 2: earnings quality. The Dec 2025 quarter profit (Rs 347 crore consolidated) looks like change-order or provision-related income, and management said in Aug 2026 it expects no write-backs this year. Other income is 35-42% of PBT.
- Suspected 3 (minor document mismatch): docs/2026-10_ppt.txt p.7 shows H1 FY27 standalone PAT as "288.35" (Rs Mn) while the quarters on p.7 sum to Rs 2,883 Mn (Rs 288 crore). It is a unit typo in the presentation, not a business issue.
- Concentration: 75% of the order book is consultancy, 56% overseas; one Kenya order is about 22% of the total order book (Dangote, a single client group across Nigeria, Ethiopia, Kenya). Middle East is "still very grim".

## Valuation (not scored)
- Market cap Rs 17,561 crore, price Rs 312, P/E 19.6 on TTM consolidated profit Rs 897 crore (facts.md). That TTM includes the unusual Dec 2025 quarter.
- On the H1 FY27 run rate (Rs 158 crore + Rs 197 crore, so Rs 355 crore, about Rs 710 crore a year), the P/E is about 25.
- Management gave no profit guidance. Its revenue target is about Rs 4,200 crore for FY27 at 16% operating margin, and Rs 5,000 crore for FY28. Market cap to TTM sales is about 4.6x; price to book about 5.3x (book value Rs 59.4).
- Plain words: the price assumes profit stays around Rs 700-900 crore and grows with the Rs 5,000 crore FY28 target. It does not assume a sharp margin fall. If margins revert toward FY25 levels (OPM 17%), the stock looks fully valued.
- Dividend yield 1.6%.

## Bull case / bear case
- **Bull:** Rs 20,420 crore order book, 75% in high-margin consultancy, runs for 3-5 years. Zero debt, strong cash, institutions buying. If H2 turnkey picks up and consultancy margin stays above 24%, FY28 profit can move beyond Rs 900 crore.
- **Bear:** Recent profit is flattered by other income and one-off change orders. Margins are at a 5-year high in a cyclical, government-linked sector. Revenue misses management's own targets, order conversion is slow (execution about 20-30% of order book a year), and one Kenya order carries much of the backlog.

## I'm wrong if
- FY27 standalone revenue grows less than 10% (below about Rs 4,200 crore), or Q3/Q4 FY27 turnkey revenue stays below Rs 300 crore a quarter.
- Consultancy segment margin falls below 22% for two quarters, or consolidated OPM (ex other income) falls below 15%.
- Kenya (Dangote) order is delayed, cancelled or its revenue recognition does not start by Q4 FY27.
- Government stake falls below 51% through an OFS, or the Supreme Court claim goes against EIL.
- Debtor days go above 55.
