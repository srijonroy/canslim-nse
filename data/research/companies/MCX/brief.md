## The business in 5 lines
MCX is India's commodity derivatives exchange with **over 98% share of futures and options turnover** (2026-11_ppt p.10). It earns transaction fees on futures (on notional) and options (on premium), so revenue follows trading activity and volatility in gold, silver, crude and natural gas.
Gold + silver are 66% of futures turnover, crude 14%, gas 10%, copper 7% (2026-11_ppt p.10).
FY26 revenue Rs 2,302 crore, profit Rs 1,332 crore. H1 FY27 operating revenue Rs 1,502 crore (+101% YoY), EBITDA Rs 1,206 crore (75% margin), PAT Rs 868 crore (+116%) (2026-11_ppt p.4). Q2 alone: sales Rs 800 crore, profit Rs 455 crore (facts.md).
Since last year: silver 100 g, gold 10 g, electricity futures (75% market share, ADT Rs 77 crore), far-dated futures, a coal/minerals exchange (platforms due Oct 2026), and possible FPI access to gold and silver.

## Red flags
None confirmed. **Checked:** audit opinion, emphasis of matter, related parties, penalties, show-cause notices (annual report, 2 Oct); SGF accounting (the Q2 pause was a SEBI circular rationalising the requirement, 2026-11 transcript p.10).
**Suspected / watch:**
- Margin flattered by the SGF pause (above); H2 margin could fall back to about 70-72%.
- Regulatory risk: SEBI or RBI can change trading economics quickly (bank guarantees in July; CPO futures still suspended per the Aug call).
- Competition: an analyst said a rival's H1 ADT "jump" with retail catching up; management declined to comment (p.21-22). Share is still 98%.
- Technology/cyber: capacity doubled, data-centre plan under review, management calls cyber "heightened focus".
- Costs: technology and CME licence fees are rising (JP Morgan question p.9); tech cost is permanent.

## Valuation (not scored)
Market cap Rs 94,857 crore, price about Rs 3,720, P/E 52.7 on TTM profit Rs 1,799 crore (facts.md). On H1 annualised profit (Rs 868 crore x 2 = Rs 1,736 crore) the P/E is about 55; on Q2 annualised (Rs 455 crore x 4 = Rs 1,820 crore) about 52. Market cap is about 31x TTM sales (Rs 3,056 crore).
The price assumes profit stays at about Rs 1,800 crore and then grows for years, even though the last 4 quarters came in a historically high-volatility stretch (FY25 profit was Rs 560 crore). The stock has already gone from about Rs 3,204 (2 Oct) to Rs 3,720, so the Q2 beat is in the price.

## Bull case / bear case
- **Bull:** A 98% monopoly with 75% margins, no debt, and a client base that nearly doubles every year. New products (power, silver 100 g, far-dated futures, coal, indices) and an FPI opening add volume without competitors taking share.
- **Bear:** Earnings are a bet on commodity volatility, now at a high. SGF contribution will restart, tech costs are rising, premium per rupee of notional has halved. A calm quarter or a regulatory cap could cut profit 30-40% and a P/E of 50 does not allow for that.

## I'm wrong if
- Q3 FY27 sales fall below Rs 650 crore.
- EBITDA margin drops under 68% once SGF contribution restarts.
- Traded clients fall for two quarters in a row (Q2: 15.0 lakh, Q1: 13.7 lakh).
- SEBI restricts retail commodity options.
- A rival exchange takes more than 5% of options turnover, or a serious trading outage occurs.

## Questions only you can answer (scuttlebutt)

- Is silver 100 g or the micro options driving retail growth, and will that last if silver prices calm down?
- Does the rival exchange's crude/energy contract draw any real liquidity in your trading screens?
- Check NSE shareholding for MCX: mutual funds and FPIs adding or trimming?
