## The business in 5 lines
MCX is India's commodity derivatives exchange: **99.07% of commodity futures turnover and 98.83% of commodity options** in FY26 (annual_report_2026, MD&A). By contracts traded it's the world's largest commodity options exchange and the 4th largest commodity derivatives exchange.
It earns transaction fees on futures (on notional turnover) and options (on **premium** turnover), so revenue rises with trading activity, especially **volatility** in gold, silver, crude and natural gas.
FY26 revenue ₹2,302 crore (2×) and profit ₹1,332 crore (2.4×); Q1 FY27 revenue ₹702 crore (+88% YoY, **−21% QoQ**), EBITDA margin 72% (facts.md; 2026-08 transcript p.2).
New areas: silver 100 g futures, electricity contracts, a coal exchange (subsidiary incorporated with SEBI approval), indices (BULLDEX being reworked), and possibly foreign investor (FPI) access to more contracts (2026-08 transcript p.2–3, p.6).

## Valuation (not scored)
Market cap about ₹81,500 crore (₹3,204 a share). On TTM profit of ₹1,542 crore that's a **P/E of about 53**; on Q1 annualised (₹413 crore × 4 = ₹1,652 crore) about **49×**.
These earnings come from a high-volatility year. If volatility (and option premiums) falls back to FY25 levels, profit could fall sharply while the business itself is fine. The price assumes participation keeps compounding.

## Red flags
None found. **Checked:** audit opinion, emphasis of matter, related parties, penalties (only rule references), show-cause notices (none in the annual report).
**Watch:** regulation (RBI and SEBI can change the economics of trading quickly) and technology outages, which have hit MCX before.

## Bull case / bear case
- **Bull:** A 99%-share monopoly with 72% margins, no debt, client numbers doubling every year, and several new products (power, coal, indices, a possible FPI opening). India's commodity derivatives market is still young.
- **Bear:** Earnings are tied to commodity volatility, which just peaked. Option premiums already fell by about two-thirds. Regulators can cut activity overnight (bank-guarantee rule, contract suspensions, F&O curbs for retail). At about 50× peak-ish earnings, a normal year could mean a lower share price.

## I'm wrong if
- Q2 FY27 revenue falls below ₹550 crore (i.e. the bank-guarantee rule and falling volatility hit harder than management says).
- Traded clients shrink for two quarters in a row.
- SEBI restricts retail commodity options (like the 2024 equity F&O curbs).
- A serious trading outage happens.
