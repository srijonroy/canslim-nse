## The business in 5 lines
Raghav Productivity Enhancers makes **silica ramming mass**, the lining rammed into induction furnaces that melt steel; it gets replaced after a number of heats. It's the world's largest maker, with 14% of the Indian market (2026-09_ppt p.10, p.17).
It sells it as a "productivity enhancer" mixed for each plant, at 1.5–2× the price of local commodity suppliers, because more heats per lining means 3–5% more furnace output for a cost below 0.5% of a plant's running costs (2026-09_ppt p.14–15).
FY26: 332k tonnes (+29%), ₹257 crore revenue, ₹55 crore profit (+48%); exports 24% of volume to 40 countries; one plant in Rajasthan (2026-04_ppt p.7; 2026-09_ppt p.19).
Next: capacity +30% (414k → 534k tonnes) due Oct 2026, an 80:20 JV in East India with TRL Krosaki (a Nippon Steel subsidiary), foundry-grade and tundish products, and research into high-purity quartz (2026-09_ppt p.25–30).

## Valuation (not scored)
Market cap about ₹8,300 crore (₹1,809 a share). On TTM profit of ₹63 crore that's a **P/E of about 132**; about 29× TTM sales.
Even if profit keeps growing 40–50% a year, it would take about 4 years for the P/E to fall to ~30 at today's price. **The price already assumes years of fast growth, with no numeric guidance from management to anchor it.** This is the main reason to hold back despite a strong checklist.

## Red flags
None found. **Checked:** promoter pledge (none), promoter stake (steady), related parties (audit-committee approved), audit opinion (clean), dilution (bonus only since FY21).
**Watch:** no earnings calls and no numeric guidance, so the business can only be judged by its reported numbers.

## Bull case / bear case
- **Bull:** A real niche leader with pricing power (charges 1.5–2× and margins are rising) and real volume growth (+29–38%). +30% capacity arrives now, with an East-India JV with a Nippon Steel company to follow. No debt, clean governance, and very little ownership by institutions, so there's room for them to discover it.
- **Bear:** At 132× earnings, any slowdown hurts the price a lot. Demand depends on induction-furnace steel, which is cyclical. Inventory and cash conversion are weak. Management gives no numbers, so there's no early warning before a miss.

## I'm wrong if
- The new capacity isn't live by Dec 2026, or utilisation falls below 75% after commissioning.
- Quarterly volume growth drops below 15% for two quarters in a row.
- EBITDA per tonne falls (a sign pricing power is fading).
- Operating cash flow stays below 60% of PAT in FY27.
