## The business in 5 lines
SBC Exports (Mirzapur, UP) started as a carpet business in 1991 and now runs three unrelated businesses: **garment manufacturing**, **IT support services and manpower supply to government bodies**, and **travel and tours** through a wholly owned subsidiary, Mauji Trip Ltd (annual_report_2026, "At a glance", business segments).
FY26 consolidated revenue was ₹403 crore (standalone ₹301 crore, so the travel subsidiary adds about ₹100 crore) and profit ₹25 crore (annual_report_2026, directors' report).
Market cap about ₹2,775 crore on ₹81 crore of book equity.
The CAN SLIM rules pass because reported profit growth is high from a tiny base, and RS is 96.

## Valuation (not scored)
Market cap about ₹2,775 crore. On TTM profit of ₹38 crore that's a **P/E of about 73**; price-to-book about 34. Over a third of pre-tax profit is other income, so the P/E on operating profit alone is far higher.

## Red flags
1. **Earnings quality**: other income is 30–71% of pre-tax profit.
2. **Debt** grew from ₹0 to ₹227 crore in five years, about 2.8× equity.
3. **Promoter stake down 14 points** in a year with no new share issue in FY26, so promoters probably sold. Verify in SAST filings.
4. **Related-party purchases** (Korporate Bizmax) approved for five years ahead.
5. **Money out of the core business**: warrants in other companies, property advances, a travel subsidiary.
6. **No disclosure**: no calls, no guidance, no current presentations.

## Bull case / bear case
- **Bull:** Revenue and profit are growing fast from a small base, and momentum (RS 96) is strong.
- **Bear:** The profit is partly other income, funded with rising debt, spread over unrelated businesses, with related-party deals and promoters apparently selling. This is the profile of a stock the rules pass on numbers but that tends to break down.

## I'm wrong if
- The next annual report shows other income below 10% of pre-tax profit, with debt flat or falling.
- An institution (mutual fund) takes a meaningful stake.
